Subject: Trademark question: commercial game "WikiGOLF" on Steam

Hello,

I am an independent developer in Japan preparing to release a PC game called "WikiGOLF" on Steam. A free demo is planned first, followed by a paid version. I would like to ask whether my planned use of the Wikipedia name and related designs requires a trademark license, or needs to be changed.

About the game:
- It builds 3D golf courses from Japanese Wikipedia articles. Players move between articles by hitting the ball into holes placed on article links.
- Article text and links are fetched at runtime through the Wikimedia APIs. Images are limited to CC0 and public domain files.
- Text is attributed under CC BY-SA 4.0 with the article title, its URL, and a notice of changes.
- It is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Wikimedia Foundation.

Elements I would like you to review:
1. The game title "WikiGOLF".
2. A self-made golf ball with a puzzle-piece pattern, shown on the title screen (screenshot attached). It is a golf ball rather than a globe, but I would like to confirm that it is not too similar to the Wikipedia logo.
3. A title screen panel styled like an encyclopedia article (screenshot attached).
4. The word "Wikipedia" used in the attribution, in a "Go to Wikipedia" link on the title screen, and in the store description to explain the game's data source.

My questions:
1. Does any of the above require a trademark license?
2. If so, how should I apply, and what would you recommend changing?
3. Would the following notice be acceptable? "WikiGOLF is an independent game and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Wikimedia Foundation."

The store page is planned to be submitted for review in [date]. An answer before then would help me avoid changing the title or artwork later.
